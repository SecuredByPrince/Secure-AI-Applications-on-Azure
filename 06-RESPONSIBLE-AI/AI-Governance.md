# AI Governance

## Overview

This document defines the AI governance framework, operational accountability model, governance lifecycle strategy, policy enforcement architecture, and Responsible AI oversight controls implemented within the Secure AI Applications on Azure project.

Enterprise AI systems increasingly influence:
- business operations
- customer interactions
- security workflows
- knowledge retrieval
- automated decision support
- operational analytics
- enterprise governance processes

Large Language Models (LLMs) and generative AI systems introduce operational and behavioural risks involving:
- unsafe outputs
- adversarial manipulation
- governance violations
- behavioural inconsistencies
- biased outcomes
- information disclosure
- operational misuse
- accountability gaps

Unlike traditional deterministic software systems, enterprise AI systems:
- dynamically generate outputs
- interpret contextual prompts
- adapt behaviour probabilistically
- rely on orchestration workflows
- introduce behavioural uncertainty

Without governance frameworks, organizations may struggle to:
- maintain accountability
- enforce AI policies
- validate operational integrity
- manage behavioural risks
- support regulatory readiness
- ensure Responsible AI adoption

This project demonstrates how enterprise AI governance can improve operational resilience through:
- governance accountability
- behavioural oversight
- operational monitoring
- policy enforcement
- risk management
- Responsible AI controls
- layered security architecture

The implementation integrates governance controls with:
- Azure AI Foundry
- Azure OpenAI Service
- Azure AI Content Safety
- Prompt Shields
- Microsoft Sentinel
- governance monitoring
- behavioural telemetry analytics

The environment prioritizes:
- accountability
- operational trust
- governance maturity
- behavioural safety
- transparency
- enterprise AI resilience

# Why AI Governance Matters

Traditional governance models generally focus on:
- infrastructure management
- policy enforcement
- access governance
- compliance oversight

Enterprise AI systems introduce additional behavioural complexity because:
- outputs are probabilistic
- prompts influence behaviour
- contextual interpretation changes dynamically
- adversarial manipulation is possible

Without governance controls:
- unsafe AI behaviour may emerge
- accountability gaps may increase
- operational trust may decline
- governance violations may occur

AI governance improves:
- operational accountability
- behavioural visibility
- governance maturity
- AI operational resilience
